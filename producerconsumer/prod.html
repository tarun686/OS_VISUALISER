Producer-Consumer Problem

The Producer-Consumer problem is a classic synchronization scenario in operating systems. It involves two types of processes:

  • Producer – generates and adds items to a shared buffer.
  • Consumer – removes and processes items from that shared buffer.

The goal is to coordinate these processes to ensure that:

  • The producer does not add to a full buffer.
  • The consumer does not remove from an empty buffer.
  • They do not access the buffer simultaneously to avoid race conditions.

Algorithm Overview

The synchronization is typically managed using semaphores or mutexes to ensure mutual exclusion and proper signaling between the producer and consumer.

Pseudocode:

          Initialize:
              mutex = 1
              full = 0
              empty = buffer_size
          
          Producer:
              wait(empty)
              wait(mutex)
                  produce_item()
                  add_item_to_buffer()
              signal(mutex)
              signal(full)
          
          Consumer:
              wait(full)
              wait(mutex)
                  remove_item_from_buffer()
                  consume_item()
              signal(mutex)
              signal(empty)

This ensures that the producer waits if the buffer is full, and the consumer waits if the buffer is empty. Mutual exclusion guarantees that only one process accesses the buffer at a time.
